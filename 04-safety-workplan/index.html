Section 04

Generation of Safety Workplan

These prompts draft a starting workplan structure (timelines, focus areas and responsibilities) that you then adjust to your unit's real schedule and hazard profile.

For the best results, upload or attach the relevant document (a recent inspection finding, an existing workplan or the applicable regulation) before you prompt, so Copilot works from your actual source rather than general knowledge.

Prompt templates

Draft a period unit safety workplan
Act as a unit safety officer. Context: I'm planning the safety workplan for [unit/department] covering [time period, e.g. "Q2 2026"]. Key focus areas are [list 2-4 areas, e.g. "vehicle safety, working at height, heat injury prevention"]. Task: draft a workplan with monthly milestones, responsible parties (use placeholder roles), and success indicators. Format: a table with columns Month, Focus Area, Activity, Responsible Role, Success Indicator.
List your real focus areas. The more specific they are, the less editing you'll need afterwards.
Build a unit safety workplan around a known gap
Act as a unit safety officer. Context: a recent inspection found [describe the gap, e.g. "inconsistent use of fall-arrest harnesses on scaffolding"]. Task: draft a 90-day corrective workplan to close this gap, including training, inspection checkpoints, and a review date. Format: a numbered list of phases, each with objective, actions, and timeframe.
Feed in the actual finding from an inspection report for a workplan that's grounded in a real gap rather than generic best practice.
Turn a safety workplan into a briefing summary
Act as a unit safety officer. Context: [paste your draft workplan]. Task: summarise this into a one-page briefing suitable for presenting to a commanding officer. Format: an executive summary paragraph, then three bullet sections: Priorities, Resourcing, Risks if delayed.
Handy once a workplan draft exists and you need a short version for a briefing rather than the full document.
Copilot doesn't know your unit's actual staffing, budget or calendar. Treat generated timelines and responsible-role placeholders as a structure to fill in, not a finished schedule.
